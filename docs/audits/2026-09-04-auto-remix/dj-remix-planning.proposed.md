# DJ Remix Planning

Governing rules revised September 13, 2026 after owner audition. These rules define acceptance; they do not certify the existing output or replace listening approval.

## Product intent

Auto Remix produces a streaming-length club alternate. Keep the recognizable hook on Drop 1; transform the arrangement, groove, and energy. Festival builds and drops are the default direction. Verses remain coherent passages. The September13 owner review requires upbeat interior passages with a present lead and a rhythmic drive; intro/outro may relax. A breakdown may vary density, but should not turn the body into indistinct background music. SFX alone do not constitute transformation.

For 2–5 songs, select one club bed and rotate compatible guest hooks. Drop 1 uses hook-replace: guest lead in, bed lead out. Drop 2 introduces exactly one clear additional idea, usually a different hook or the bed's returning chorus. Remaining songs earn complete cameos after Drop 1 or are skipped.

Root `AGENTS.md` continues to own tempo caps, one-kick pulse eligibility, DSP/gain requirements, analysis confidence, testing, and the completion standard. Do not restate contradictory exceptions here.

## What makes a good join

Evaluate seven properties: musical placement, beat lock, recognizable complete lyric, one low-end owner, continuity, tension/release, and a clear reason for the change. A blend can be gradual or an anticipated hard cut. Smoothness means coherent rhythm and phrasing. A title token is not a mandatory cut point: choose the full musical phrase that produces the more natural handoff. A title near the END of a chorus identifies that containing section, not its entrance. Prefer a confident measured vocal-section start on the source bar grid; retaining a consonant pickup does not prove the chorus or line is complete.

Protect complete lines and pickup syllables. Word-onset gaps alone do not prove a phrase ended. Use measured vocal offsets, sustained-note tails, lyrics where available, and musical section evidence with confidence. A quiet valley is not permission to shorten a scheduled lyric, and a fixed two-bar extension does not prove the next boundary is legal. When extending a continuation, check its new exit and protect the incoming pickup. A vocal rest need not become a quiet musical runway: keep a prepared instrumental groove through it. Never assume source time zero is beat one. Annotate phrase origin separately from bar downbeats.

Select both outgoing and incoming boundaries before assigning the join time. Prefer a later legal boundary or a different incoming passage to an after-the-fact overlap of two unfinished lead lines.

## Arrangement and timing

Use intro → complete A hook → transition → Drop 1 → breakdown → build → Drop 2 → short outro as a useful starting shape. The owner authorized musical judgment beyond prior recipes on September 13: phrase integrity, a coherent groove and a purposeful energy arc outrank effect quotas or a forced runway. Aim for Drop 1 after 16–24 elapsed bars. Choose the intro duration around a complete, recognizable hook; do not automatically append a long verse before it. Preserve at least eight bars of identity and prefer 16 for a full chorus when the source supports it. A complete measured nine-bar hook must not be cut to eight or padded to16 with a quieter verse solely to fill a slot. Preserve its measured span, and keep atmospheric head/tail treatment outside the retained hook.

The first drop must resolve to a verified musical phrase entrance. A two-bar transition occupies a musically valid lead-in; it must not blindly shift an otherwise correct phrase entrance by two bars. The independently annotated early-drop fixture must land after 16–24 elapsed bars with no exception. For other sources, a later drop may serve a complete phrase or a more natural arrangement. Record the source evidence and musical reason; do not mark it auditioned or independently approved merely because the planner supplied an explanation. Test the annotated early-drop case and legitimate long-hook cases separately.

Low confidence reduces structural cutting. It does not eliminate the club energy arc. Avoid guessed section labels, forced cameos, or arbitrary cuts to satisfy a quota.

## Transition selection

Use a compact repertoire within the existing effects and SFX palette:

1. **Short rising handoff:** keep the outgoing phrase coherent, add a modest continuous rise over roughly one or two bars, and transfer to a naturally placed incoming phrase. A deliberate outgoing continuation may remain. Let it finish, then delay the following song when needed; do not solve rapid successive cuts by deleting that continuation. The September 12 owner review specifically preferred keeping the problem-is-this passage. Do not insert a mandatory title-token preview. Prepare the incoming groove without competing lead vocals or low end, and preserve its first complete line. A hard cut is an option when musically justified, not a universal requirement.
2. **Phrase overlap with low-end handoff:** for compatible non-pivot groove joins, provide actual temporal overlap and coordinated gain curves. One deck owns kick and bass; the outgoing yields as the incoming takes over. Equal-power is a starting point for uncorrelated material; verify correlated sources do not swell or cancel.
3. **Brief echo take-out into an intentional cut:** when sustained blending would clash, finish the outgoing line, apply one justified decaying throw, and enter on a legal beat. Tail ownership must not mask the new lead. Keep the current restrictions on non-pivot voids.

Measure the instrumental backing separately from the lead. A loud vocal can conceal a missing kick, bass or harmonic bed. Compare the lead-in and the entire following backing together, including weak short windows and the end of the following hook; do not choose the loudest isolated beat or normalize only the first title word. Preserve measured bar phase when selecting a backing island. A complete four-bar instrumental phrase may be looped when an eight-bar backing contains a breakdown; this does not shorten the foreground vocal identity stay. Score the lead-in too, so a repair does not relocate the same dropout. Keep the same backing balance through subsequent loops. If the outgoing backing falls away, a compatible incoming instrumental passage may start earlier and continue into the drop while the outgoing lyric finishes. Replace the outgoing low-end owner; do not layer another kick. Missing stems or unmeasured source intervals cannot authorize this repair.

Choose transition duration from the available musical runway. Use one coherent handoff for Drop 1; avoid a short lyric detour followed immediately by a second cut. A small rise is the default, with denser festival effects reserved for material that supports them. Do not apply an identical effect package to every pair solely because it passes a fixture.

Constant-rate one-beat grain wallpaper and verbatim repeated passes are retired. Repetition must develop the arrangement. Historical recipes belong outside active instructions.

Input handling must have a defined outcome for short clips, duplicate track identities, unknown tempo, malformed timing and more than five songs. Validate times before constructing beat grids. Missing measured beat/downbeat evidence cannot authorize structural cuts merely because tempo and key metadata are present. Never privilege a named reference pair in role selection; test the same evidence under neutral display names.

The applied timeline must preserve the validated musical decision. Do not reinsert an effects package or manufacture a diagnostic claim because an old regression expects particular effect names. Assert the actual placed events, timing and coverage instead. A backing-drum entrance is not a transfer of vocal ownership. When extending a continuous passage, absorb any old tail already covered by that passage; never replay it over the new lead. Preserve incoming pickups through every repair pass. A later weak word-gap repair cannot overrule a verified phrase/bar exit. Remove already-covered duplicate continuations before generic overlap repair can damage the retained lead.

## Vocal, kick, bass, and effect ownership

Write an explicit owner timeline for lead vocal, kick, bass, and supporting harmony. Never stack two full-mix drops. Optional call-and-response needs complementary phrasing and a bounded span; it is not the default Drop 1 treatment.

With sidecars, use real vocal and instrumental stems. With partial/missing stems, do not silently substitute a full mix for a missing vocal or drum stem and then claim isolation. Choose a compatible handoff or degrade the overlay. Role proxies indicate likely content; they do not separate it.

Existing blur is a low-pass. It does not remove kick/sub and does not remove a vocal from a full mix. Do not label it high-pass or treat it as proof of low-end ownership. Use permitted stem/arrangement/gain controls and verify the rendered bands. Add no new effect types or on-device separation runtime.

Thin-song pulse follows the root rules. Strong drums prohibit a second kick, even if a full-mix bass or drum proxy is uncertain. Contradictory evidence lowers confidence.

## Tempo and gain

Every sustained guest hook (8–16 bars) must reach the actual shared tempo within its allowed rate, including a documented half/double beat mapping if used. An incompatible guest may instead contribute a native-speed, non-repeating chop of at most one mix beat and 0.75 seconds, whichever is shorter; at most two such chops per eight bars, only after Drop 1 in a breakdown or outro, outside another lead phrase. It cannot supply kick/bass, establish an identity stay, replace a required full hook, or evade a harmonic gate. If isolation and a clean word boundary are unavailable, skip the chop. Full 8–16-bar cameos use the sustained-hook rules. A clamp that misses the target is not success. Preserve the root's ≤12% gentle midtempo lift and normal vocal/bed limits. Apply the same approved mapping to cuts, stems, playback, and export.

Set per-clip gain from measured local loudness and stem makeup. A bounded level rise may preserve groove through a vocal rest; derive it from the actual outgoing layers, ramp it, and carry the resulting balance through the following passage. Do not count removed bed vocals in the reference or let a later staging pass erase the selected gain. Recheck foreground vocal balance whenever the backing changes. Compare voiced local windows to that song's native vocal/backing contrast; avoid silence-driven gain estimates. A louder bed cannot count as a successful repair if lyrics recede. Keep adjustments bounded and smooth, preserve complete phrases, and treat missing evidence as unavailable. Preserve at least 6 dB of pre-SFX mix-bus headroom and approximately −1 dBTP on output. Festival SFX belong in the transition/drop and must leave the hook intelligible. Calibrate their combined gain and ducking; never equate more effects with a better join.

Preserve phrase dynamics and intentional subtraction. A quiet region is acceptable only with source and arrangement evidence, not because it lies inside an exempt label. Avoid raising an entire stem solely to improve speech-recognition scores.

Verify decoded delivery audio as well as the internal master. An AAC encode can overshoot a compliant PCM master; use bounded attenuation and recheck the delivered file. An encoding check does not substitute for loudness, limiter, ownership or listening evidence.

## Rendered automation

Represent trajectories once and use them in live and export rendering. Do not simulate a smooth filter sweep merely by assigning two abrupt per-bar targets. Preserve continuity and effect state across segment boundaries. Use inaudible anti-click handling without consuming the incoming syllable. A continuous consonant pickup may precede the drum downbeat; it must retain the same source-to-timeline clock and may not grow on repeated validation. Distinguish this from a repeated preview of a later title token. Verify overlap and tails on actual PCM.

After repair, revalidate phrase boundaries, ownership, tempo, gain, SFX, and source continuity together. A repair must not make the next transition illegal.

## Evaluation and reporting

Use three layers: deterministic structural/PCM tests, real-DSP local-song regressions, and perceptual audition. Synthetic fixtures need independent rhythmic, bass, harmonic, and vocal-like content. Sine waves alone cannot certify musical quality.

Record complete manifests: working-tree identity, audio/stem/analysis hashes, canonical plan, renderer/settings, seed, every join, expected lyric spans, and measured results. Missing or inconclusive required evidence is not pass. Test negative controls and held-out songs; prevent empty conditional checks from passing unnoticed.

A nonzero waveform is not sufficient evidence of audible continuity. The owner-rejected September 12 r3 excerpt has a severe shallow dropout that passed the digital-silence test. Keep it as a perceptual failure reference, use the frozen short-window loudness gate, and review the end of each listening excerpt as well as the cut itself. Do not boost silence or drown a gap in SFX. The owner later accepted the brief September13 foreground-candidate dip but rejected prolonged quiet and entry near the chorus ending. Preserve that distinction: acceptance of a small transition is not permission for a long unexplained lull or a partial hook.

Audio-model critique may add timestamped observations, but it must be calibrated against known faults and owner ratings. Transcription, CLAP descriptors, and RMS are not equivalent to DJ judgment. Report which observations were measured, inferred, model-assessed, or heard by a person.


A model that misses a blinded injected dropout is not a qualified transition judge. Do not accept its claim that a pause is intentional without the source/arrangement evidence. Keep its output advisory, record the failed control, and avoid spending repeated cloud calls on uncalibrated approval. The September 8 calibration is recorded in the audit implementation evidence.

Before completion, provide: complete-hook and phrase evidence; Drop 1 timing; actual realized tempos; role/low-end ownership; transition reason and alternatives rejected; every join's measurements; source-continuity checks; SFX and limiter behavior; live/export parity; blind comparison outcomes; and headphone/speaker reviewer sign-off. Preserve unresolved findings as unresolved.

Acceptance windows and numerical limits are frozen in `docs/audits/2026-09-04-auto-remix/acceptance-contract.json`. Any later revision requires independent reference evidence, a version change, and rerunning the baseline and negative controls; never relax a gate simply to fit a candidate.
